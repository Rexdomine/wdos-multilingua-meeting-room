1. Welcome

Welcome to the WODDI Volunteer Leadership Activation Programme. Over the next fourteen days you will learn about the organisation you are preparing to serve, demonstrate your leadership through a series of light activities, and take your first steps toward a leadership role in one of our networks. This manual is your Day One reading. Its purpose is simple: by the time you finish it, you should understand what WODDI is, what our networks do, how our leadership is organised, and what will be expected of you as a volunteer leader. Read it fully and without rushing. Everything you do during activation will make more sense once you understand the whole. A note before you begin Every leadership position in WODDI is voluntary. There is no salary and no stipend. People serve because they believe in raising women and young women across Africa, and because the service itself develops them as leaders. If that purpose speaks to you, you are in the right place.

2. About WODDI

The Women of Divine Destiny Initiative (WODDI) is a pan-African, women-led organisation committed to empowering women, strengthening families, nurturing young people, and uplifting communities across Africa. WODDI was established in 2008 and is registered in Nigeria under the Companies and Allied Matters Act. It was founded by Her Excellency Zinaria Nneoma Nkechi Rochas Okorocha, PhD. WODDI is known by its enduring identity: WODDI — The Nurturer. Everything the organisation does flows from that idea — nurturing potential in women and young women until it grows into leadership, service, and lasting impact. Vision and purpose WODDI exists to see African women and young women rise into their divine destiny — confident in their identity, equipped with skills and character, active in leadership and service, and shaping their families, communities, and nations for good. Who WODDI serves • Women and mothers — through the WODDI Good Mother Network (WGMN). • Girls and young women — through the WODDI Nurture NextGen Network (WNNN). • Women leaders across Africa — through the WODDI Inspiring Women Summit (WIWS) and the wider programme family. How WODDI works WODDI operates across Africa through a structure of volunteer leaders, supported by digital platforms including the WODDI Institute learning app and a central membership and coordination system. Programmes are delivered mostly virtually and at low or no cost, so that a woman’s location or income never decides whether she can take part.

4. The Two Networks You May Lead

WODDI’s two flagship membership networks are WGMN and WNNN. You are being activated to serve in one of them. Understanding both helps you see how they work together — mothers in one, their daughters’ generation in the other, forming one continental family. WGMN — The Good Mother Network WGMN serves women and mothers. It strengthens the woman at the heart of the family — her identity, her wellbeing, her skills, and her leadership — so that stronger mothers build stronger homes and communities. Its leaders carry the title Representative. The four WGMN pillars Identity Nurture & Formation Affirming who she is — her worth, calling, and Growing character, wellbeing, and capacity. belonging. Leadership & Service Influence & Legacy Leading in the home, community, and network. Shaping her world and the generation behind her. WNNN — The Nurture NextGen Network WNNN serves girls and young women, generally aged eighteen to thirty. It roots young women in identity and values, builds their character and practical skill, and raises them into leadership and influence — giving them genuine responsibility rather than symbolic roles. Its leaders carry the title Lead. The four WNNN pillars Identity Formation Belonging, confidence, and self-worth. Character, discipline, and values. Leadership & Service Influence & Impact Real responsibility and visible platforms. Shaping schools, campuses, and communities. One difference to remember WGMN leaders are called Representatives; WNNN leaders are called Leads. This is a deliberate distinction. Use the correct title for your network.

Both networks share the same four-level continental structure. Strategy flows downward from the continental level to the community; insight, testimony, and results flow back upward. You will serve at one of these levels. 6. The Leadership Roles Explained

This section describes what each leadership position does. You may be activated for any of these. Read the one you are applying for closely, and the others so you understand how the whole network holds together. Country Representative (WGMN) / Country Lead (WNNN) The most senior volunteer leader in a country. Sets the national direction for the network, appoints and supports lower-level leaders, drives membership growth, represents WODDI to national partners, and reports country progress upward to continental leadership. Works closely with a Deputy who shares the load and ensures continuity. Deputy Country Representative / Deputy Country Lead Partners the Country Representative or Lead, steps in when they are unavailable, and often carries specific responsibilities such as membership, programmes, or communication. The Deputy is a genuine second-in- command, not a title in name only. State / Regional Representative or Lead (and Assistant) Coordinates the network across a state, province, or region. Supports chapters and local leaders, facilitates regional gatherings and programmes, mentors chapter leaders, and carries directives down while feeding regional insight back up. The Assistant supports and deputises. LGA / District Representative or Lead (and Assistant) Leads the network within a local government area or district — the bridge between regional coordination and the community chapters. Establishes and supports chapters, tracks membership and activity locally, and ensures programmes actually reach the grassroots. Chapter Lead (and Assistant Chapter Lead) Leads a single chapter or circle — the heartbeat of the network, where members gather, grow, and serve. Runs the weekly and monthly programme rhythm, cares for members, welcomes and orients new members, keeps the chapter active, and celebrates member milestones. This is where the mission becomes real. Chapter Communication Lead Manages all chapter communication — group administration, sharing of official materials, posting weekly programme content, coordinating engagement, and reporting chapter activity upward. This role keeps information flowing and the chapter connected. Campus Ambassadors (WNNN) Young women who represent WNNN within a university or institution — forming and growing campus chapters, running the youth programme suite, and drawing fellow students into the network. Campus Ambassadors are central to how WNNN grows. What every role shares • Every role is voluntary and rooted in service. • Every role has a leader and a deputy or assistant — no one carries it alone. • Every leader recruits members, delivers programmes, communicates, and reports. • Every leader upholds WODDI’s values and safeguarding standards without exception.

7. What Is Expected of a WODDI Leader

Leadership in WODDI is earned through service and sustained through consistency. Whatever your level, the following are expected of you. Commitment • Give the time your role genuinely requires, and be honest about your availability. • Follow through on responsibilities even when no one is watching. • Stay engaged through the network’s regular rhythm of meetings and activities. Character and conduct • Uphold WODDI’s values and Code of Conduct at all times. • Treat members, fellow leaders, and communities with respect and dignity. • Protect confidentiality and disclose any conflict of interest. Safeguarding • Put the safety and wellbeing of members — especially young women and minors — above all else. • Never ignore or mishandle a safeguarding concern; follow the reporting procedure and escalate immediately. • Keep all interactions visible, accountable, and appropriate. Communication and reporting • Respond promptly and keep members and leaders informed. • Submit the reports your role requires, on time and with evidence. • Carry information faithfully — downward from leadership and upward from the community. How WODDI recognises its volunteers Because roles are unpaid, WODDI rewards service through recognition, development, and belonging — certificates and digital badges that build your profile, free access to WODDI Institute learning, a continental network of peers, opportunities to be seen and heard, and a clear path to grow from volunteer to Ambassador, Fellow, and continental leadership. Consistent service is seen, valued, and rewarded.

8. The Fourteen-Day Activation Ahead

This manual is your Day One reading. Over the coming fourteen days you will move through a light, structured programme that helps WODDI understand your strengths and place you where you will thrive. It is not an examination to be feared — it is how we get to know you. Across the two weeks you will complete short tasks that reveal how you think, communicate, plan, and lead. Some are quick knowledge checks; some ask you to write or plan; some observe how you work with others. Completing each step on time is itself part of what we look for, because reliability is the foundation of volunteer leadership. At the end of the fourteen days, your activation is reviewed and you are recommended for placement, for placement with mentoring, or for a role better suited to you. Whatever the outcome, you will have grown, and you will understand WODDI far better than you do today. Your first step Read this manual in full. Let it settle. When you understand what WODDI is, what our networks do, and what leadership here asks of you, you are ready to begin Day One of your activation with confidence. Welcome to WODDI.
